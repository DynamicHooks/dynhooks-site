Economics now

Why rules should change

A launch is a bet made in one transaction. A market has a lifecycle.

Every launchpad so far asks you to set the rules once and live with them. Today the moments below are handled off-chain, by multisig votes, by manual contract calls, or not at all. Dynamic Hooks moves them into the hook.

At launch

Nobody is verified and nobody has liquidity. The right thing to do is protect the pool and the people who show up early.

The creator proves who they are

They should be paid directly, not through an escrow or a promise.

The market gets deep

The fee that made sense at ten ETH of liquidity is a tax at a thousand.

The community hits something real

A product shipped, a number reached, a vote passed. The economics should be allowed to reward it.

Programmable tokens put rules in the asset. Programmable markets put rules in the venue.

How a market is born

Three things, one transaction.

The launcher pays a small creation fee in ETH and signs once. No presale, no allocation to the launcher, and no way to seed the pool with less than the full supply.

A token

Fixed supply, minted once, no mint function, no owner. The default is 1,000,000,000 units.

A pool

A Uniswap v4 pool against ETH, with the whole supply placed as a single-sided position and locked in a contract that has no withdraw function.

A program

The list of rules that govern the market's economics, chosen from a template and filled in by the launcher.

Programs and rules

A program is an ordered list of rules.

The hook reads the market's economics on every swap: the fee rate, the routing table, the liquidity mode, the rewards switch. Rules change those values. Nothing else can.

What can fire a rule

Verified(event)

A signature from an allow-listed verifier, bound to this market, with a nonce and a deadline. The creator owns the handle; the team hit a public milestone.

State(metric op value)

Read from the chain at evaluation time. Liquidity ≥ 50 ETH; cumulative volume ≥ 1,000 ETH; 30 days since launch; holders ≥ 500.

Agent(key)

A call from an allow-listed agent address, and the rule's own State or Verified check still passing.

Vote(threshold)

An on-chain signal from holders of the market token. A later release.

Agents execute; they do not decide. An agent can make a true rule fire sooner. It cannot make a false rule fire at all.

What a rule may change

SetFee(bps)0 to 300. Never above 3%.
SetRouting(recipients, shares)Shares sum to the fee; recipients only from the market's declared set.
RouteToEscrow / RouteToCreatorThe claim switch. The escrow is reachable only by its claim.
ActivateRewards / DeactivateRewardsTurns the holders' share on or off. Accrued balances are never clawed back.
SetLiquidityMode(mode)locked or locked_plus_buyback. Removal is not a mode.
ReleaseIncentive(amount, to)From the market's pre-funded incentive vault only, capped per rule at creation.
Anyone can call evaluate. If the condition holds, the action is applied in that transaction and a RuleFired event is emitted. If it does not hold, the call reverts and nothing changes.
What cannot change

Three things no program can do.

They are not in any template, cannot be added by any program, and are not parameters. They are properties of the engine.

01

Remove liquidity

The locking contract has no such function. Not paused, not time-locked, not governed: absent.

02

Reach an escrow without its claim

The escrow moves on a verified claim, or to the holders after the window. Nothing else.

03

Set a fee above 3%

The hook refuses it at the engine level.

Who gets paid

Every trade pays a fee, inside the swap.

The fee is taken on the quote side by the hook and distributed in the same transaction. A market declares its recipients at launch. A program can move shares between them. It cannot add a seventh.

Creator

The person or team the market is about. Paid directly once verified; held in escrow until then.

Launcher

The wallet that created the market.

Holders

Everyone holding the market token, by balance and time, through the holders pool.

Protocol

Dynamic Hooks. Funds buybacks and operations; pays referral links from its own share.

Referral

The wallet whose link brought the trade, paid from the protocol share.

Escrow

The creator's share while unverified. Keyed to the creator's identity, not to any wallet.

Quote in a stock. Robinhood Chain carries Robinhood Stock Tokens. A market can use one as its quote asset instead of ETH, so a market about a company's founder pays its creator, its holders and its launcher in that stock on every trade. It ships after ETH-quoted markets.

Buybacks

A published share of the protocol's net fee income buys $DHOOKS on the open market and burns it. A contract does it; anyone can call it. It is how the protocol spends part of its revenue, not a floor under anything.

Governance

Holders decide which templates are added, which quote assets are allowed, which verifiers are on the list, and parameter changes within published bounds.

Later, weight

A future release lets $DHOOKS be staked to increase a wallet's weight in the holders pools of the markets it holds, up to a published cap.

$DHOOKS comes first. The token comes before the protocol so the build can be funded in the open. Until markets open, holding it is holding a position in the plan, and plans can fail.

Straight talk

Read this before anything else.

Markets can go to zero.

The program changes economics; it does not create demand. Most markets anywhere end at zero.

Rules are only as good as their conditions.

A Verified rule trusts a verifier's signature. A State rule trusts the chain. If you would not trust us to sign, do not launch a market that depends on our signature.

Verification is not endorsement.

A creator proving a handle means the money goes to them. It does not mean they asked for the market, like it, or will ever acknowledge it.

Questions

Short answers.

Who can fire a rule?

Anyone. Calling evaluate on a rule whose condition holds applies the action in that transaction. If the condition does not hold, the call reverts and nothing changes. The protocol runs an agent that evaluates State rules; anyone else can run one too.

Can the launcher change the rules after launch?

No. The rule is written at launch. The change happens later, automatically, when the condition is met and proven. The launcher does not hold a key to it, and neither does the protocol.

What can a verifier do, and not do?

It can delay a true claim by refusing to sign. It cannot move funds, change economics directly, or sign for an event the program did not define. If a verifier goes dark, the allow-list is updated and the claim proceeds with another.

What happens to the creator's share if they never show up?

It accrues in an escrow keyed to the creator's identity. It moves in exactly two cases: the creator proves the identity and claims it, or the claim window passes and it is paid to the holders pool. It is never burned.

Does the launcher get tokens at launch?

No. The entire supply is placed as one single-sided position in the pool. The launcher puts in nothing but the creation fee, holds nothing at launch, and cannot have pre-bought. The first buyer pays the starting price.

Do I need $DHOOKS to use the protocol?

No. You do not need it to launch, trade, hold, verify or evaluate anything.
